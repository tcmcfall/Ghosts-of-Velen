# Cape Velen — Campaign Canon

## Campaign frame

- Campaign year: **1492 DR** unless a later date is explicitly selected for a scene.
- The campaign is an open-world, proactive roleplaying setting adapted from *Ghosts of Saltmarsh* into southwestern Faerûn.
- Player goals drive play; prepackaged adventure plots do not dictate the campaign.
- Rules preference: **5.5e** = *Player's Handbook* (2024), *Dungeon Master's Guide* (2024), *Monster Manual* (2025).
- A class is a class, not a people. A Noromathian druid is a Noromathian elf of the druid class; a Noromathian ranger is a Noromathian elf of the ranger class.

## Names and geography

- **Duchy of Cape Velen** is the state.
- **Velen** is the capital. There is no city named "Cape Velen."
- **Dragon's Neck Peninsula** is the canonical geographic name; older references to Dragon's Head/Neck are normalized to Dragon's Neck in GoV prose unless quoting a source.
- Cape Velen presently operates on the **two-county model**: **Firedrake County** and **Fyraven County**.
- **The Bite** replaces the Hool Marshes adaptation: a haunted, marshy coastline north of Saltmarsh forming the dragon's maw.
- **Burle is removed** from the Cape Velen adaptation.
- Important Cape Velen locations developed in the Volo itinerary include Velen, Saltmarsh, Tulmene, Monguldarath, Oakbottom, Jhaansciim, Honorguard House, Shoremeet, Tordraken, the western Wealdath, and Woodfaire.
- Directional frame: northwest points toward civilization, ports, commerce, and the Moonshaes; north/east increasingly toward war vigilance and Muranndin; south toward Saltmarsh, fishing, and Firedrake Bay; the interior belongs to the Wealdath and its elven history.

## Cultural identity

Cape Velen carries a strong **Scottish Highlander** social tone without reducing the people to costume or caricature. Veleans are generally direct, clear-spoken, practical, proud, resilient, and impatient with empty ceremony or dandified self-importance. Competence matters. Promises matter. Work matters. The maritime tradition is foundational.

The duchy has Norse influences as well, especially in maritime culture, but the dominant developed voice is the gruff, capable Highlander spirit.

## Historical frame

Cape Velen was historically part of Tethyr's western/northern provincial structure and played an important naval role against pirates, corsairs, and reavers of the Nelanther Isles.

The campaign history subsequently diverges from published history:

- Cape Velen became independent from Tethyr.
- **Duke Calchais** was the ruler immediately before Aldric Thorne in the current approved Volo continuity.
- Earlier three-county formulations are superseded in current continuity. The lost third county belongs to older history and was conquered by **Muranndin** before the present reclamation era.
- Muranndin's rise devastated northern Tethyr, the Wealdath, and the Dragon's Neck.
- Muranndin conquered the mountainous southern borders of Amn, burned and occupied huge swaths of the Wealdath, displaced Noromathian communities, and subjugated nearly half the Dragon's Neck Peninsula, reaching almost as far west as **Monguldarath**.
- Muranndin's armies include orcs, hobgoblins, ogres, giants, and other monstrous forces. Their occupation is intentionally treated as a dark, brutal threat comparable in narrative weight to Mordor: militarized, cruel, oppressive, and spiritually destructive.
- Muranndin troops have a reputation for extraordinary cruelty toward civilians, including enslavement and atrocities.

## The Night of Blood

In **1480 DR**, Admiral **Aldric Thorne**, commander of Cape Velen's fleet and a veteran of the Nelanther campaigns, concluded that Duke Calchais could not or would not save the peninsula.

Aldric raised a cadre of loyal Marines, sailors, officers, nobles, and sympathizers and stormed the ducal palace in Velen. The coup was **not bloodless**. Fighting continued through the following days and hundreds died. Support for Aldric swelled as fear, misery, and anger against the failing regime spilled into open revolt.

When the smoke cleared, Calchais was dead and Aldric Thorne wore the ducal crown.

The event is remembered as **the Night of Blood** (with the understanding that its violence extended beyond a single literal night).

Aldric declared Cape Velen reborn and replaced the old livery with **ivory and gold**, centered upon the golden dragon Aurumandur. The duchy's flag depicts the golden dragon protecting/encircling a ship: the dragon protects the fleet, and the fleet protects the peninsula.

## Aurumandur, the Thousand Truths

**Aurumandur** is an ancient gold dragon philosopher, approximately two thousand years old in the developed tradition. She is famous—or infamous—for the philosophical work **The Thousand Truths**.

The Thousand Truths is **not inherently scripture or law**. It is a philosophical treatise concerned with existence, leadership, justice, duty, conflict, restraint, responsibility, and perception. Its intended thematic character combines Taoist-style reflection with strategic overtones reminiscent of *The Art of War*.

The writings themselves are not revolutionary in every proposition; their influence comes from longevity, breadth, ambiguity, and application. Wild and contradictory interpretations have occurred throughout history. Rulers, priests, soldiers, merchants, judges, and revolutionaries have all found different lessons in the same text.

Key developed philosophical motifs include:

- Truth is discovered, not invented.
- Every decision costs something.
- Strength exists to protect, never merely to dominate.
- The sea remembers arrogance.
- Hope is labor, not optimism.
- Listen first; judge second.
- Even enemies teach.
- The greatest victory is the one never fought.
- Refusing to act can itself be a consequential choice.

Aldric is deeply spiritual and personally devoted to the principles he reads in the Thousand Truths. He is a **lawful good paladin**. His devotion is central to understanding both his altruism and the frightening certainty with which outsiders may view his regime.

## The Rebirth

Aldric's political program is commonly described as **the Rebirth**.

He does not intend merely to hold what remains of Cape Velen. His government intends to reclaim the Dragon's Neck, rebuild lost settlements, restore roads and trade, strengthen the fleet, and form durable alliances capable of resisting Muranndin.

The Duke openly recruits settlers: farmers, craftsmen, merchants, sailors, soldiers, scholars, refugees, and adventurers willing to build lives in Cape Velen. Supporters view this as necessary repopulation and enlightened frontier policy. Critics describe it as recruitment of ideological true believers into a young militant state organized around Aurumandur's teachings.

This controversy is deliberate. From outside Cape Velen, the regime can plausibly look near-messianic.

## Foreign relations

- **Amn** is the only regional power currently established as formally recognizing Aldric Thorne and his regime.
- Other states may trade or communicate with Cape Velen without formally normalizing relations.
- Tethyr does not accept the political situation as settled.
- The youth, militancy, revolutionary origin, territorial ambitions, and Aurumandur-centered philosophy of the regime all contribute to foreign hesitation.

## Noromathians and Suldusk

The Wealdath has multiple elven histories and identities. Do not collapse them into a single generic "elf" culture.

- The **Noromathians** are the principal elven allies involved in Aldric's modern reclamation effort.
- Their alliance with Cape Velen is relatively new, roughly two decades in the broader developed setting, and based on mutual strategic need and growing trust rather than timeless harmony.
- Humans are not automatically welcome deep within sacred Wealdath territory.
- Muranndin's invasions have displaced many Noromathians and intensified the need for western refuges and reclaimed territory.
- The **Suldusk** are a distinct historical elven people associated with the Dragon's Neck/Wealdath history. Earlier work distinguished surviving Suldusk/wild-elven identity from Noromathian identity. Preserve that distinction; do not merge them casually.

## The Wealdath in 1492 DR

The western Wealdath is **not** presented as pristine Rivendell-like wonderland.

The elves were driven from much of this western reach centuries ago. Without wardens, the natural order fractured. Recent Muranndin invasions have physically and spiritually scarred the forest and introduced or reintroduced monsters and species previously absent or eradicated.

The result is ancient, haunted, wild, forgotten, and genuinely sinister.

The 35–40 mile crossing from Tordraken toward Woodfaire might be a day's hard travel on an open road. Through the Wealdath it takes roughly **two days and two nights**, with arrival on the third day. The emotional arc matters: the first two days should feel oppressive enough that Volo genuinely fears for his life. Near Woodfaire on the third day the forest becomes perceptibly lighter, both literally and spiritually.

## Woodfaire

Woodfaire embodies Aldric's experiment without being a utopia.

- It enjoys meaningful local autonomy and is governed by a local council in a manner comparable to Saltmarsh.
- Its allegiance to the duchy is not seriously contested; the settlement exists and thrives because of the alliance and the Duke's policy.
- Humans and Noromathians coexist in a deliberately integrated frontier community.
- Elven architecture is organic, intricate, grown/pruned rather than conventionally built, with homes high among living trees and rope bridges between them.
- Human architecture is more angular and crafted but often incorporates living trees into its core, appearing both above and below the canopy.
- Commerce is everywhere, especially at ground level: craftsmen, merchants, tax collectors, farmers, ranchers, halflings, dwarves, humans, and elves.
- Soldiers are ubiquitous because the wilderness outside the settlement is extremely dangerous **and** because human-elven coexistence still generates friction.
- Miscommunication, old grievances, prejudice, and violence remain possible. The military presence helps preserve civil order as well as defend the frontier.

## Velen

Velen is the capital and a maritime fortress-city built amid dramatic white cliffs.

Important developed POIs include:

- Sea Gate — gateway to the harbor.
- Wood Gate — road into the Wealdath.
- Drake Gate — eastern military gate.
- Captain's Cliff — wealthy captains and the ducal palace; commands a sweeping view over the city and harbor.
- Widows Hill — old homes and older stories.
- Scrimshaw Lane — fine carvings and stranger folk.
- Riptide Walk — promenade beside the sea.
- Shipsgrave Tower — associated with Umberlee's shadow.
- Dolphin's Leap — gentler maritime religious face.
- Millvuthan — old haunted estate.
- Seven Cuts Court — dangerous after dark.
- Blackthorn's Brew — Volo's established lodging; ancient and tied to Blackthorn in source history.

The road south toward Woodfaire winds through the cliffs. Initially the traveler passes between high, straight walls of white stone; farther south those walls seem gradually to meet the road as it disappears toward the interior.

### The ghosts of Velen

The ghosts are a defining feature of daily life and derive from the city's ancient catastrophic tidal wave.

They are **visible**, not merely sensed. Think of them as an everyday spectral population woven into the city rather than a succession of jump scares.

Most are trapped in loops:

- a final action,
- a daily routine,
- a mundane errand,
- a conversation,
- or, in some cases, a horrifying final moment during the disaster.

Most are oblivious to living people. Some can be interacted with. Most interactive spirits remain mundane or harmless. A small number are genuinely malevolent.

Veleans have adapted. The ghosts are part of civic memory and ordinary life.

## Saltmarsh adaptation

- *Ghosts of Saltmarsh* Saltmarsh is integrated into Cape Velen.
- **Gellan Primewater** remains the wealthy Saltmarsh merchant and party-giver. Volo attends one of Gellan's famous parties and develops a detailed first impression of the host and guests.
- **Sea Princes** are replaced in the wider adaptation by the **Corsairs of Port Skaug**.
- **Scarlet Brotherhood** is replaced by the **Zhentarim**.

## Shoremeet and sea elves

Shoremeet is a lighthouse/outpost associated with contact with sea elves of Tarseth Bay and the Moonshae sphere. Relations are intentionally more strained and culturally uncertain than Cape Velen's relations with the Noromathians. Contact should feel new, fragile, and capable of misunderstanding rather than like a mature diplomatic alliance.

## Oakbottom

Oakbottom is associated with halfling immigrants from the Purple Hills and a strong whiskey tradition. It provides one of the warmer, more domestic contrasts within Volo's journey.

## Current story thesis

Cape Velen is not meant to be proven perfect.

Aldric's lesson to Volo ultimately becomes: **you cannot change what is; you can guide it and hope it follows. Good intentions will fail. There is no pain-free life. The price must still be paid. Reach for the moon knowing you may fail, because in reaching you may nevertheless find yourself among the stars.**

Woodfaire's murder and the Wealdath's death of Moonbrook exist specifically to prevent the Rebirth from becoming propaganda or utopia. The project is hopeful precisely because hope persists in the presence of failure, grief, danger, and moral cost.
