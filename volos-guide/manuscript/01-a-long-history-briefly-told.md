# Chapter One
## A Long History... Briefly Told

*"No traveler truly understands a land by merely walking its roads. One must first understand why those roads were built, who fought to defend them, and whose bones now lie beneath them. Fortunately for both author and reader alike, I shall endeavor to condense nearly two thousand years of history into but a few pages. Historians may grumble. Travelers, I suspect, will be grateful."*

— **Volothamp Geddarm**

### The Western Frontier

For most of its history, the Dragon's Neck Peninsula was never intended to stand alone.

Its western shores, blessed with abundant fisheries and deep natural harbors, supplied much of Tethyr's maritime trade, while the towering forests of the Wealdath furnished timber that became some of the finest ships ever launched upon the Sea of Swords. The people who settled these rugged shores became sailors almost by necessity. The sea fed them, enriched them, and just as often buried them.

To the west lay the Nelanther Isles.

Few stretches of water in Faerûn have earned such an infamous reputation. For centuries, pirates, corsairs, and reavers sailed from those countless rocky islands, descending upon merchant shipping with predictable regularity. The Duchy's predecessors answered in kind. Generation after generation of Velean captains hunted the sea lanes, escorting merchant convoys and engaging corsair flotillas in battles that became the stuff of local legend.

It was a hard life.

It also forged a hard people.

Long before the peninsula produced great rulers, it produced remarkable sailors.

Every family seemed to boast a captain, a Marine, or a fisherman who had stared down storms that would send wiser folk running for shore. Courage was admired. Competence was expected. Titles mattered considerably less than whether a man—or woman—could be trusted when the wind turned foul.

For centuries, Cape Velen's greatest enemy came from the sea.

It would not remain so.

### The Coming of Darkness

Barely a generation ago, the balance of power across southwestern Faerûn changed almost overnight.

From the wild lands beyond Amn's southern mountains came the Sythillisian Empire, known more simply—and with no small measure of dread—as **Muranndin**.

Its armies were unlike anything the civilized kingdoms had faced in living memory.

Orcs marched beside disciplined hobgoblin legions. Ogres hauled siege engines through forests thought impassable. Giants shattered walls that had stood for centuries. Worse still were the creatures few survivors spoke of willingly—aberrations, fiends, and horrors drawn from forgotten places whose very presence seemed to poison the land around them.

Northern Tethyr collapsed with alarming speed.

Entire towns disappeared beneath smoke and ash. Ancient roads vanished beneath marching armies. Refugees flooded westward carrying tales that many dismissed as exaggeration until the next wave of survivors arrived bearing even darker stories.

The Wealdath suffered perhaps most grievously of all.

Great swaths of the ancient forest burned.

Those that did not were twisted by war, abandoned by their wardens, or occupied by creatures that had not walked beneath those boughs for millennia. The Noromathian elves, who had protected the forest since time beyond memory, found themselves fighting a desperate guerrilla war simply to preserve what remained.

The Dragon's Neck Peninsula fared little better.

Fortresses fell.

Settlements were abandoned.

The eastern marches vanished beneath Muranndin's banners until scarcely half the peninsula remained free. The invaders advanced almost as far west as Monguldarath, and many believed the fall of Cape Velen itself was no longer a matter of *if*...

But *when*.

For the first time in its history, the peninsula feared not the sea...

But the land.

### The Night of Blood

Among those watching the slow collapse of the duchy was its Lord Admiral.

**Aldric Thorne** had spent nearly his entire life at sea. He had earned his command not through noble birth, but through decades of service fighting corsairs, protecting merchant convoys, and leading Marines in battles whose victories rarely found their way into history books. Like many throughout the duchy, he grew increasingly convinced that Duke Calchais possessed neither the resolve nor the vision required to survive what was coming.

In the autumn of 1480 DR, that conviction became action.

Marines loyal to the Admiral landed within Velen under cover of darkness. Sailors left their ships to fight beside them. A surprising number of nobles, magistrates, and ordinary citizens declared for Thorne almost immediately. What began as a carefully planned coup quickly descended into several days of bitter street fighting remembered today simply as **the Night of Blood**.

When the smoke finally cleared, Duke Calchais was dead.

So too were hundreds of his subjects.

Admiral Aldric Thorne accepted the ducal crown.

The old banners disappeared.

In their place rose a standard unlike any the peninsula had ever flown: an ivory field bearing the golden dragon **Aurumandur, the Thousand Truths**, encircling a proud Velean ship beneath full sail.

The symbolism was unmistakable.

Cape Velen had not merely changed rulers.

It had declared itself reborn.

Within months, the new Duke accomplished what generations of diplomacy had failed to achieve. Through mutual respect and shared purpose, he secured a formal alliance with the Noromathian elves of the Wealdath, whose legendary ranger known simply as **Foxfire** became one of his closest allies in the struggle against Muranndin.

To some, Aldric Thorne had saved a dying nation.

To others...

He had merely replaced one certainty with another.

The debate continues.

### Cape Velen Today

Today's Cape Velen is a nation simultaneously ancient and astonishingly young.

Its greatest wealth still comes from the sea. Fishing fleets sail daily from Velen, Saltmarsh, and countless smaller villages, while the peninsula's shipyards remain among the finest in the western Sea of Swords. Timber from carefully managed forests, Oakbottom whiskey, wool, livestock, worked stone, and finished ships comprise the duchy's principal exports. In return arrive steel, fine cloth, spices, books, glassware, luxury goods, and countless necessities from Amn, Calimshan, the Sword Coast, and beyond.

War, however, has become every bit as important to the economy as trade.

Shipwrights build warships as readily as merchant vessels. Blacksmiths fashion armor before ornaments. Roads, bridges, and fortifications receive constant attention. Nearly every family counts someone among the Marines, the fleet, or the frontier garrisons.

Politically, Cape Velen occupies an equally precarious position.

Amn, recognizing the strategic importance of a stable western frontier, has formally acknowledged Duke Aldric's government and maintains cordial relations. Elsewhere, recognition remains elusive. Tethyr has never accepted the loss of its western province nor the legitimacy of Thorne's revolution. Many neighboring realms prefer cautious diplomacy, quietly trading with the duchy while carefully avoiding any action that might appear to legitimize its government.

Meanwhile, the Duke pursues a policy that many consider his boldest—and most controversial.

Across Faerûn, settlers are openly invited to Cape Velen. Farmers willing to reclaim abandoned fields. Craftsmen prepared to rebuild ruined villages. Merchants seeking new markets. Scholars, adventurers, refugees, and soldiers alike are promised opportunity, purpose, and a place within what the Duke calls **The Rebirth**.

Supporters hail the policy as visionary.

Critics whisper that it amounts to something altogether different: the careful gathering of men and women already sympathetic to the teachings of Aurumandur, gradually transforming Cape Velen into a nation bound together not simply by law, but by shared conviction.

As with so much concerning Duke Aldric Thorne...

The truth likely lies somewhere between.

### Volo's Commentary

It was this curious little duchy that extended an invitation while I was enjoying a considerably quieter stay in Baldur's Gate.

By then I had heard every imaginable opinion concerning Cape Velen. Some described a miraculous frontier reborn through courage and vision. Others warned of a militant state led by a charismatic revolutionary whose followers quoted the writings of an ancient dragon with near-religious devotion. Nearly everyone agreed upon only one point:

Something extraordinary was happening upon the Dragon's Neck Peninsula.

Whether that extraordinary thing would prove to be history's next great success...

Or its next great mistake...

Remained to be seen.

I therefore resolved, as any sensible scholar would, to travel there myself.

After all, it is considerably more difficult to misunderstand a people once one has broken bread with them.

With that decision made, I boarded passage bound eventually for the white cliffs of Velen...

Quite unaware that the Duke had already begun teaching me my first lesson.
