# Introduction

> **Working manuscript note:** This captures the latest approved direction from the recovered conversation. It should remain concise in final layout. The emphasis is Muranndin -> Night of Blood -> revolutionary regime -> Volo's suspicion.

Barely a generation has passed since the birth of **Muranndin**, the dark empire that forever changed the face of southwestern Faerûn.

From the wilds beyond Amn's southern mountains came the Sythillisian horde, an army unlike any the region had faced in living memory. Orcs, hobgoblins, ogres, giants, trolls, and creatures darker still swept across northern Tethyr with astonishing speed, burning ancient forests, enslaving entire villages, and reducing centuries of civilization to ashes. Vast reaches of the Wealdath—once among the oldest and most sacred forests in Faerûn—were consumed by fire or corrupted beneath the boots of occupation. By the time the invasion finally slowed, nearly half of the Dragon's Neck Peninsula had fallen beneath the banners of Muranndin, its armies advancing almost to Monguldarath itself. To many, it seemed only a matter of time before the peninsula would disappear forever.

Then came **the Night of Blood**.

Convinced that Duke Calchais had neither the will nor the ability to save his people, Admiral Aldric Thorne gathered a cadre of loyal Marines, sailors, officers, nobles, and citizens who shared his conviction. Together they stormed the Ducal Palace in Velen. What began as a coup became several days of savage fighting. Hundreds died. Yet when the smoke finally cleared, it was Aldric Thorne who wore the ducal crown.

The old banners were lowered.

New standards rose above the palace, harbors, and fortresses of Cape Velen.

At their center was not the crest of an ancient noble house, but the golden dragon **Aurumandur, the Thousand Truths**.

Aurumandur is no ordinary heraldic beast. For nearly two thousand years the ancient gold dragon has been remembered as philosopher, teacher, mediator, and author of the sprawling treatise known as **The Thousand Truths**. The work is neither scripture nor law, but a collection of reflections upon existence, duty, leadership, justice, conflict, perception, and restraint. Its meditative character is balanced by a soldier's fascination with strategy: understanding before action, victory without waste, strength restrained by purpose. Its propositions are not always revolutionary. Its interpretations sometimes are. Kings, priests, merchants, generals, judges, and revolutionaries have quoted Aurumandur with equal certainty while reaching remarkably different conclusions.

Aldric Thorne declared Cape Velen reborn beneath that philosophy.

Lost settlements would be reclaimed. The Noromathians of the Wealdath would become allies rather than strangers beyond the trees. Muranndin would be driven from the Dragon's Neck. Farmers, craftsmen, merchants, sailors, soldiers, refugees, scholars, and adventurers from across Faerûn were invited to settle the frontier and become part of what the Duke openly called **the Rebirth**.

His supporters call it salvation.

His critics see something considerably more troubling: a young, militant duchy born in revolution, led by a charismatic paladin with an almost messianic certainty of purpose, recruiting new citizens already sympathetic to the teachings of an ancient dragon. Amn alone among the nearby powers has formally recognized the new regime; elsewhere, merchants may trade and diplomats may speak, but governments remain cautious about treating Aldric's crown as legitimate.

It was against this backdrop that Duke Aldric Thorne's invitation found me while I was staying in Baldur's Gate, far to the north.

I confess that I hesitated.

History has supplied no shortage of charismatic leaders convinced that they alone possess the wisdom to reshape the world. From a comfortable distance, Cape Velen appeared perilously close to becoming another such tale.

If the rumors were true, the story deserved telling.

If they were false...

Then perhaps the truth deserved it even more.

And so I departed for Cape Velen, journal in hand and skepticism firmly in tow, determined to discover whether Duke Aldric Thorne's bloody revolution had truly given birth to a brighter future...

Or merely another tragedy dressed in noble intentions.
