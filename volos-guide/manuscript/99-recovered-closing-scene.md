# Recovered Closing Scene — Leaving Cape Velen

> This is the recovered long-form closing prose developed in the original Volo conversation. Preserve it as manuscript material. It can be edited later, but its narrative beats and final metaphor are approved continuity.

## Leaving Cape Velen

Every journey must eventually surrender itself to memory.

Mine ended much as it had begun.

At the harbor.

The following morning dawned crisp and brilliantly clear, the Sea of Swords unusually calm beneath a pale autumn sky. Palace servants quietly assisted me in packing the astonishing collection of journals, maps, sketches, and assorted curiosities that somehow seem to accumulate whenever I travel. Before long, a small detachment of Velean Marines arrived, their young lieutenant politely informing me that His Grace had ordered an escort to the docks.

I protested that I was perfectly capable of finding my own way through the city.

The lieutenant smiled.

*"So are we."*

I wisely accepted.

The streets of Velen were only beginning to awaken. Bakers opened their shutters. Fishermen prepared their boats. Merchants arranged their stalls beneath the watchful gaze of centuries-old ghosts quietly repeating familiar morning routines. The city already felt strangely familiar to me.

That realization surprised me more than I cared to admit.

When we reached the harbor, my frigate lay ready to depart upon the morning tide.

To my greater surprise...

So did the Duke.

There he stood upon the quay, entirely without ceremony, speaking comfortably with half a dozen elderly sailors gathered beside a stack of coiled hawsers. Judging by the laughter, they were discussing something considerably more amusing than affairs of state. One old bosun slapped the Duke squarely upon the shoulder while another pointed accusingly toward the harbor, prompting a chorus of good-natured disagreement that would have scandalized many royal courts.

No one bowed.

No one announced his presence.

For a few moments...

He was simply another old sailor enjoying old stories among old friends.

Only when one of the Marines quietly indicated my arrival did Aldric glance over his shoulder.

He smiled.

Excused himself with an easy nod.

Then walked toward me alone.

*"Master Geddarm."*

*"Your Grace."*

For a moment neither of us spoke.

The sounds of the harbor carried comfortably between us—the cries of gulls, the creak of rigging, and the steady rhythm of sailors making ready to put to sea.

Finally, the Duke broke the silence.

*"I owe you an apology."*

I confess...

That was not the sentence I expected.

*"I fear I rather... commandeered your travels."*

There was the faintest hint of amusement in his eyes.

*"I suspect there were moments you questioned my intentions."*

I laughed despite myself.

*"There were moments, Your Grace, when I questioned your sanity."*

He nodded thoughtfully.

*"A fair assessment."*

We shared the sort of laugh that only arrives after a difficult journey has safely concluded.

Then his expression became quieter.

*"I appreciate your patience."*

He looked toward the bustling harbor before continuing.

*"I asked much of you."*

*"You did."*

*"I offered very few explanations."*

*"You did."*

*"I hope..."*

He paused.

*"...you understand why."*

I looked past him toward the city climbing the white cliffs.

Toward the road disappearing south.

Toward the hidden forests beyond.

Toward places that now carried names, faces, and memories instead of mere marks upon a map.

*"I believe..."*

I answered slowly.

*"...I do."*

He nodded once.

Entirely satisfied.

Remarkably...

He never asked what I intended to write.

Not one word.

No request for favorable treatment.

No attempt to influence my account.

No careful reminders of hospitality shown.

He simply regarded me for several long moments with those thoughtful gray eyes that always seemed to weigh a man's character more carefully than his words.

Then he smiled.

It was not the smile of a duke.

It was the smile of an old admiral seeing another ship safely under way.

*"I wish you fair winds, Master Geddarm."*

*"And I you, Your Grace."*

He turned to leave.

Then stopped.

*"Oh..."*

I looked up.

*"When your book is finished..."*

The faintest trace of mischief crossed his face.

*"...might I trouble you for a signed copy?"*

I assured him that such a request would be among the easiest I had received during my entire stay.

He nodded toward the palace high upon Captain's Cliff.

*"For the Duchess, of course."*

There was just enough pause before those final three words that I could not help but laugh.

*"So long as she promises to overlook the chapters concerning your remarkable talent for manipulating itinerant scholars."*

His grin broadened.

*"I make no such promises."*

With that, he tipped two fingers toward his brow in a sailor's farewell and returned to his waiting companions upon the quay, where the conversation resumed as though it had never been interrupted.

No fanfare.

No procession.

No royal farewell.

Simply...

An old sailor returning to his friends.

As the frigate gathered way and the white cliffs of Velen slowly receded into the morning haze, I remained upon the quarterdeck watching until the harbor had nearly disappeared from sight.

Only then did the final lesson reveal itself.

The Duke had never sought to persuade me.

He had never attempted to purchase my admiration.

He had never once asked me to tell the world that Cape Velen was worthy of notice.

He had simply opened his doors.

Introduced me to his people.

Trusted them to speak for themselves.

I had thought myself the guest.

I now realized...

I had been the invitation.

Somewhere, months from now, a merchant, scholar, wandering adventurer, refugee, or dreamer would open my latest volume and read of haunted harbors, stubborn halflings, lonely frontier keeps, impossible forests, and a little village called Woodfaire where hope still dared to grow.

Some of them, inevitably, would come.

Some would trade.

Some would settle.

Some would simply carry those stories farther still.

Standing upon the quarterdeck, watching Cape Velen disappear beyond the horizon, I found myself smiling.

Duke Aldric Thorne had never stopped inviting new people into his duchy.

He had merely found another ship.

And this time...

**Its sails were made of paper.**
